Multi-Label Topic Classification of News Articles is a comprehensive end-to-end NLP research project that addresses the challenge of automatically assigning multiple relevant topic labels to news articles scraped from real-world sources, BBC News and The Guardian. Modern news content frequently spans overlapping domains such as politics, economy, technology, health, climate, and international affairs simultaneously, making traditional single-label classification approaches insufficient and limiting the effectiveness of content organization, analytics, and recommendation systems at scale. To solve this, the project builds a fully modular pipeline that begins with web scraping approximately 97,000 raw articles from both sources, followed by thorough preprocessing that includes HTML tag removal, deduplication, whitespace normalization, lowercasing, tokenization, and binary multi-label vector encoding, ultimately producing a clean, high-quality dataset of around 92,500 articles ready for transformer-based training. The labeling system covers 12 standardized topic categories — Politics, Economy, Business, Technology, Health, Climate & Environment, Science, Education, Sports, Crime & Law, International Affairs, and Society — with each article capable of carrying multiple labels simultaneously, encoded as binary vectors to align with multi-label classification objectives. Three transformer architectures were benchmarked across this dataset using PyTorch and Hugging Face Transformers: DistilRoBERTa-base achieved 91% validation accuracy with lightweight efficiency, BERT-base-uncased provided a stable 91% baseline, and RoBERTa-base delivered the best overall performance at 92% accuracy with superior contextual representation across all label categories. A two-stage training strategy was employed where Stage 1 trained the model on the full dataset to learn general topic distributions, followed by Stage 2 fine-tuning on the cleaned and preprocessed subset to improve accuracy and address label imbalance across less frequent categories. Custom data loaders and tokenizers were built to handle multi-label batch construction efficiently, ensuring consistent label distribution across training, validation, and test splits throughout the entire training workflow. The project is evaluated using multi-label F1-score, precision, and recall metrics, with label-wise performance analysis revealing strong results on high-frequency categories like Politics and Economy while noting slightly lower recall on niche labels such as Climate & Environment and Society, with confusion analysis confirming the model accurately predicts overlapping topic combinations like Politics + Economy and Technology + Science. RoBERTa-base was identified as the best-performing architecture due to its deeper bidirectional contextual encoding, while DistilRoBERTa offered a compelling trade-off between accuracy and computational efficiency suitable for resource-constrained environments. This project demonstrates strong research rigor by combining large-scale dataset construction, rigorous data quality checks, state-of-the-art transformer fine-tuning, and thorough multi-label evaluation methodology into a cohesive system designed to power data-driven news intelligence and automated article categorization at scale
